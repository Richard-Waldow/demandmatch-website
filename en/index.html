Run the numbers yourself.
With your figures, not ours.

Example for a distributor with €1.2bn revenue, 12% gross margin, stock at 12% of revenue and 400 people in sales. Conservative (0.5% revenue, 0.1 points, 3.5% of stock): €2.4m a year. The license is priced on that: €90,000 base plus 15% of €2.4m, about €446,000 a year from year two and about €268,000 in year one. With setup (€60,000) and own effort (€32,000), year one costs about €360,000. Payback after about 3.6 months, 3-year ROI about 370%. Expected (1% revenue, 0.3 points, 10.5% of stock): €6.4m a year with the same license, payback after about 1.3 months, 3-year ROI about 1,180%. You keep 81 to 93% of the value. On top, 10 to 20% of sales time freed, 40 to 80 full-time people, not counted.

How we calculate
  1. Gross profit from captured requests = revenue × extra revenue % × gross margin.
  2. Margin uplift = revenue × margin points.
  3. Lower holding cost = revenue × stock % × share sold earlier × holding cost. The capital released and avoided write-downs are not counted.
  4. Value per year = (gross profit from captured requests + margin uplift + lower holding cost) × share of the business run through DemandMatch.
  5. Freed capacity = people × time freed × cost per person. It only counts if you include it.
  6. Year 1 brings 50% of the value (ramp-up), years 2 and 3 bring 100% each.
  7. License per year = base (€90,000 for the first company, €30,000 for each further one) + 15% of the conservative value, at most a third of that value. If your own assumptions are below the conservative case, yours apply. Freed capacity never counts.
  8. In year one the license counts half the value, as the ramp-up does. Setup €60,000 one-time, €20,000 for each further company.
  9. Cost in year 1 = year-one license + setup + own effort (days × €800). Years 2 and 3 = license. The pilot is credited to year 1.
  10. 3-year ROI = (3-year value − 3-year cost) ÷ 3-year cost. Payback = the month in which cumulative value exceeds cumulative cost.

What you should know
about DemandMatch.

What is DemandMatch?

DemandMatch is software for B2B distribution. It matches your inventory with your customers' demand automatically: for every request from email, phone or CRM it finds the right material, warehouse and margin and prepares the offer. The other way round, it finds the customers who buy exactly what sits in overstock or arrives inbound. Your sales team reviews and approves.

Who is DemandMatch for?

For distributors and wholesalers of chemicals, plastics and industrial goods with at least $1bn in annual revenue, above all for heads of sales, field sales and inside sales teams who have to bring many requests and a lot of stock together.

What does DemandMatch bring to a sales team?

More sales, because no request is left waiting and no customer is forgotten. Better margin, because every offer accounts for freight, storage and your pricing rules, and stock that sits gets sold instead of written off. And less manual work: reading, matching and writing the offer run automatically.

Why is the price fair?

Because it follows the value we measure together. Before the pilot both sides write down how things run today and how we calculate. Only the conservative case counts, freed time never does. The price is fixed for a year, and year one counts half the value, because the ramp-up takes time. At every renewal we measure again: if the value rises, the share rises with it; if it falls, your license falls too, never below the base. And the license is never more than a third of the value, so you always keep at least three times what you pay.

What ROI can a distributor with €1bn revenue expect?

At €1.2bn revenue and 12% gross margin we expect €2.4m (conservative) to €6.4m (expected) a year from more sales, better margin and stock sold earlier. The license is priced on the conservative case only: about €446,000 a year from year two. With setup and own effort, year one costs about €360,000 and pays back in about 1.3 to 3.6 months. Freed sales time comes on top. The calculator on this page lets you adjust every assumption. Only money that actually arrives goes into the ROI; freed time is shown next to it and counted only if you choose to.

How much does DemandMatch cost?

The fit check is free. The value assessment costs €20,000 and the 30-day pilot from €60,000, depending on scope. Every paid step is credited to the next one: the value assessment in full to the pilot, the pilot 100% to year one of the license if you sign within 30 days of the review. The license follows an open formula: a base of €90,000 a year for the first company with its ERP (€30,000 for each further one) plus 15% of the annual value we measure together on your data in the value assessment and the pilot, in the conservative case only. It is never more than a third of that value, so you always keep at least three times what you pay. Setup is €60,000 one-time (€20,000 for each further company). All prices net, excl. VAT. In the US: $22,000, from $66,000, base $99,000 ($33,000 for each further company), setup $66,000 ($22,000), excl. sales tax.

Which systems does DemandMatch work with?

DemandMatch reads from the systems you already run: ERP such as SAP, Oracle, Odoo or DATEV, CRM such as HubSpot, the inbox (Outlook, Gmail) and messengers such as WhatsApp or Slack. Nothing is replaced and there is no system change.

Does DemandMatch send offers to customers by itself?

No. DemandMatch prepares, a person decides. No offer and no email goes out before your sales team has seen and approved it.

What happens to our data?

No system change, no big project. DemandMatch has read-only access to ERP, CRM and inbox and changes nothing there. Your data stays where it is. We set up the connection and access rights together with your IT.

How do we get started?

With a free fit check: you send us a few facts first (industry, revenue, ERP, size of your sales team), so we only talk where it fits. Then a value assessment on one division's data and a 30-day pilot on your real data, for one product family and one team, with success criteria agreed in writing upfront. Week 1: connect read-only. Week 2: match your real requests against your real stock, with your rules on margin and customers. Weeks 3 and 4: your team works with it, and together we measure what it brings.

Built for German companies.
Works council and data protection planned in.

Works council

In Germany, software that shows what individual employees do needs the works council's agreement. We give you an information pack: which data DemandMatch uses, who sees what, and why. We plan 4 to 8 weeks for it, alongside the value assessment.

Data protection

A data processing agreement under Art. 28 GDPR, the details for your record of processing and, where needed, the documents for a data protection impact assessment. Where the data is processed is written into the contract. Your data protection officer has it all before the pilot.

IT security

We answer your security questionnaire and show that access is read-only. We set up accounts and rights with your IT and document every connection.

Inbox

We only read the mailboxes and folders you release, such as the sales inbox. If private use of email is allowed, we clear that first with data protection and the works council.

One country first

We start where your ERP already runs, usually Germany. Other countries and newly acquired companies follow, one at a time.

Price in steps

The license grows with the scope: one country first, then the group. Each step fits your budget and your approvals, and the decision rests on measured numbers.
